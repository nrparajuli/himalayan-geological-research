2026 Rasuwa Ice–Rock Avalanche: Reconstruction of a Transboundary Ice–Rock Avalanche and Debris-Flow Cascade in the Central Himalaya

Nawaraj Parajuli · Durga Khatiwada

HGR GRAPHICAL ABSTRACTICE / ROCK AVALANCHE → IMPACT → CHANNELIZED DEBRIS FLOW → TRISHULI CORRIDOR

Abstract

This article presents a framework for reconstructing the 2026 Rasuwa ice–rock avalanche and its downstream transformation into a channelized debris-flow cascade. The analysis integrates field evidence, digital elevation modelling, geomorphological interpretation, remote sensing, river-channel geometry and flow-path reconstruction.

Keywords: Rasuwa · Himalaya · ice-rock avalanche · debris flow · Trishuli · geohazard · geomorphology · GIS

1. Introduction

High-mountain failures can evolve rapidly from an initial slope or cryospheric collapse into destructive channelized flows. In steep Himalayan terrain, interactions between ice, rock, water and confined river corridors can produce cascading hazards that extend far beyond the source area.

2. Geological and Geomorphological Setting

The study focuses on the Rasuwa–Trishuli corridor of the Central Himalaya, where steep relief, active fluvial incision and high-altitude cryospheric terrain provide the setting for complex cascading processes.

3. Data and Methods

The HGR workflow combines DEM-based terrain analysis, mapped affected extent, river-centreline reconstruction, channel-distance analysis, field observations and remote-sensing interpretation.

4. Avalanche-to-Debris-Flow Cascade

The conceptual sequence investigated here is: source ice–rock collapse → high-energy impact → entrainment and transformation → channelized debris flow → downstream propagation and deposition.

5. Flow Reconstruction

Flow reconstruction can be presented through a continuous Trishuli reference path, station sequence, chainage, travel time, estimated velocity and affected-channel extent. These parameters should be linked to the underlying GIS data for reproducibility.

8. Discussion

The final research version should distinguish direct observations from model-derived estimates and document uncertainties in source volume, effective energy transfer, channel roughness, travel time and entrainment.

9. Conclusions

The HGR reconstruction framework provides a reproducible structure for documenting cascading Himalayan hazards from source failure through downstream river response.
